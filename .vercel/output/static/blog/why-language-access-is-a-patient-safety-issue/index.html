Why Language Access Is a Patient Safety Issue

October 14, 2026·interpretingWorks

The Data Is Clear

A 2023 Joint Commission report identified communication failures as a root cause in over 70% of sentinel events. For patients with limited English proficiency (LEP), that risk compounds.

Studies show LEP patients experience:

  • 2x higher rate of adverse events vs English-speaking patients
  • Longer hospital stays due to miscommunication at discharge
  • Higher readmission rates when post-care instructions are not fully understood

The Standard of Care Has Changed

Using a bilingual family member or untrained staff member as an interpreter is no longer acceptable practice. It creates liability, violates patient privacy, and introduces clinical risk.

The standard today: a qualified medical interpreter for every encounter where language access is needed — on-site, via VRI, or OPI.

What Hospitals Are Doing Differently

Leading health systems are treating language access the same way they treat infection control: as a patient safety protocol, not an administrative nicety.

That means:

  • Flagging language need at registration, not at bedside
  • Pre-scheduling interpreters for planned procedures
  • Using remote interpreting (VRI/OPI) for urgent and walk-in situations
  • Documenting every interpreter encounter for compliance
