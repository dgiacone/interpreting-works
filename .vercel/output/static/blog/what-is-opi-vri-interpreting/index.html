OPI vs VRI: Which Remote Interpreting Mode Is Right for You?

October 14, 2025·interpretingWorks

When your organization needs an interpreter but an on-site professional isn’t available, two remote options step in: Over-the-Phone Interpreting (OPI) and Video Remote Interpreting (VRI). Both are fast, flexible, and HIPAA compliant on the interpretingWorks platform — but they serve different situations.

What Is OPI?

Over-the-Phone Interpreting connects a patient, provider, and interpreter via a standard phone call. There’s no camera required. It works on any device with a phone signal, making it the most accessible option for urgent or unscheduled situations.

Best for:

  • Urgent or walk-in appointments
  • Settings where screens are impractical
  • Languages with very high interpreter availability by phone
  • Low-bandwidth environments

What Is VRI?

Video Remote Interpreting adds a live video feed to the call. The interpreter appears on a screen, which is especially valuable when visual communication matters — facial expressions, body language, and crucially, American Sign Language (ASL).

Best for:

  • Deaf and hard-of-hearing patients (ASL)
  • Sensitive conversations where visual cues matter
  • Pediatric or mental health settings
  • When building rapport is important

How interpretingWorks Handles Both

On the interpretingWorks platform, schedulers can request OPI or VRI from the same interface. The system automatically matches your request with a qualified interpreter based on:

  • Language and dialect
  • Modality credentials (VRI requires additional certification for some languages)
  • Availability and proximity

Real-time status updates keep your team informed, and electronic check-in/out via geo-location ensures accurate billing for every session.

The Bottom Line

If you need speed and simplicity, OPI is your go-to. If the interaction requires visual communication — or involves ASL — VRI is the right choice. With interpretingWorks, switching between modalities takes seconds.
